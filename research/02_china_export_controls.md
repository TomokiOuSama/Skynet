# 2. China Export Controls & Geopolitical Risk

## 2.1 Timeline of Export Control Actions

| Date | Action | Impact on AXTI |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Jul 2023 | China restricts Gallium & Germanium exports | Indirect -- affects raw material flows |
| Dec 2024 | China tightens Germanium/Gallium/Antimony controls further | Signals escalation |
| Feb 4, 2025 | **China adds InP substrates to export control list** | **Direct hit on AXTI** |
| Feb 2025+ | Per-shipment export permits required (~60 business days) | Revenue delays begin |
| Q3 2025 | AXTI reports permit processing "improving" | Temporary relief |
| Q4 2025 | Permits delayed again; revenue drops to $25.5M (from $33M Q3) | Revenue decline resumes |

## 2.2 How Export Controls Work for AXTI

**AXTI's unique vulnerability:** 100% of manufacturing is in Beijing via subsidiary **Beijing Tongmei**.

- Every shipment of InP substrates from China requires an individual export permit
- Processing time: ~60 business days per permit (vs. normal shipping in days)
- Permits are **not guaranteed** -- government can deny or delay at will
- Creates massive uncertainty for customers who need reliable supply

**AXTI's response:**
- Applied for and received some export permits
- Exploring "alternative manufacturing arrangements" outside China (no specifics disclosed)
- CEO Morris Young stated at Needham Conference (Jan 2026) that permits are being processed but with unpredictable timing

## 2.3 Revenue Impact

| Quarter | Revenue | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Q2 2025 | ~$28M | Pre-control baseline |
| Q3 2025 | $33.0M | Permits flowing, temporary improvement |
| Q4 2025 | $25.5M | Permit delays resume |
| Q1 2026E | $24-26M | Guided range, continued constraints |

**The structural problem:** Even when permits are granted, the unpredictability makes AXTI an unreliable supplier. Customers cannot plan production around 60-day permit uncertainties.

## 2.4 Customer Behavior Changes

### International Customers (Shifting Away from AXTI)
- **One major customer signed a 7-year supply deal with Sumitomo** (per B. Riley analyst note) -- directly due to AXTI supply concerns
- Lumentum expanding internal InP production (4-5 fabs)
- Coherent vertically integrating InP substrate production
- JX Metals expanding capacity to absorb demand diverted from AXTI

### Chinese Domestic Customers (Shifting to Domestic Suppliers)
- Huawei/HiSilicon: Qualifying Yunnan Xinyao (Hubble Investment owns 23.91%)
- Innolight (中际旭创): Raised domestic InP sourcing target from 30% to 50%
- Accelink (光迅科技): Reportedly ordering 30,000 wafers/year from Xinyao

**Net effect:** AXTI is being squeezed from both sides.

## 2.5 China's Strategic Intent

China's export controls on InP serve multiple strategic purposes:
1. **Retaliation** for US semiconductor export controls (CHIPS Act, entity lists)
2. **Leverage** in trade negotiations
3. **Nurture domestic industry** by creating captive demand for Chinese suppliers
4. **Signal capability** -- China controls critical upstream materials

**The broader context:**
- China controls ~80% of global Gallium production and ~60% of Germanium
- InP substrates are a chokepoint in the AI supply chain
- These controls are unlikely to be fully reversed -- they are a strategic asset for China

## 2.6 AXTI's Options

1. **Wait it out:** Hope permits normalize. Risk: they may never fully normalize.
2. **Build manufacturing outside China:** Extremely capital-intensive ($100M+), would take 2-3 years minimum. AXTI has $47.8M cash.
3. **License technology:** Partner with a non-China manufacturer. Dilutes competitive advantage.
4. **Get acquired:** A larger company (II-VI/Coherent, Sumitomo) could acquire AXTI for its technology and customer relationships. Book value provides a floor (~$3.87/share).
5. **Spin off Tongmei:** Separate the China operations to serve Chinese market, while building new capacity elsewhere.

## 2.7 Key Takeaway

The export control situation is the single most important factor for AXTI's stock. It transforms a company with strong secular tailwinds into one with existential risk. Resolution of this issue (either through policy change, manufacturing relocation, or acquisition) is the key catalyst.
