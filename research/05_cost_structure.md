# 5. AXTI Cost Structure & Vertical Integration

## 5.1 Vertical Integration Overview

AXT is unique among InP substrate manufacturers in its **deep vertical integration**:

```
Raw Indium Ore
    ↓ (AXTI refines in-house)
7N Purity Indium (99.99999%)
    ↓ (AXTI synthesizes in-house)
InP Polycrystal
    ↓ (AXTI grows via proprietary VGF)
InP Single Crystal Boule
    ↓ (AXTI processes in-house)
Polished InP Substrates
    ↓ (Shipped to customers)
Epitaxial Growth → Devices → Transceivers
```

**Competitors like Sumitomo** also have vertical integration but through different supply chain structures. AXT's key differentiator is controlling the raw material refining step.

## 5.2 7N Indium: The Hidden Moat

### What is 7N Indium?
- 7N = 99.99999% purity (seven nines)
- Required for high-quality InP crystal growth
- Impurities at the parts-per-billion level affect crystal quality and device performance

### AXT's Advantage
- AXT refines its own 7N Indium from raw ore through subsidiary operations
- Most competitors must **purchase** 7N Indium from third-party refiners
- This gives AXT an estimated **15-25% cost advantage** on raw materials
- Also provides **supply security** -- not dependent on external 7N Indium suppliers

### Indium Market Context

- Indium is a byproduct of zinc mining -- supply is inelastic
- China's dominance in Indium refining adds another geopolitical dimension
- 7N refining is technically challenging -- few facilities worldwide can achieve it

## 5.3 VGF Crystal Growth Technology

**VGF (Vertical Gradient Freeze)** is AXT's primary crystal growth method:

**Advantages vs LEC (Liquid Encapsulated Czochralski):**
- Lower dislocation density (better crystal quality)
- Lower thermal stress during growth
- Better uniformity across the wafer
- More suitable for large-diameter (4-6 inch) InP

**AXT's VGF expertise:**
- Decades of process optimization
- Proprietary know-how on thermal profiles, crucible design, seed preparation
- This tacit knowledge is a significant barrier to entry
- Takes new entrants 5-10 years to replicate equivalent crystal quality

## 5.4 Cost Structure Breakdown (Estimated)

| Cost Component | % of COGS | Notes |
|----------------|-----------|-------|
| Raw materials (In, P, dopants) | 30-35% | Partially offset by vertical integration |
| Crystal growth (utilities, crucibles, labor) | 25-30% | High fixed cost component |
| Wafering & polishing | 20-25% | Cutting, lapping, polishing, cleaning |
| Quality control & testing | 5-10% | EPD measurement, surface inspection |
| Packaging & shipping | 3-5% | |

**Key cost dynamics:**
- High fixed costs → significant operating leverage with volume
- At current (constrained) volumes, fixed cost absorption is poor → margin compression
- At full utilization, gross margins could reach 40-45%
- Beijing manufacturing costs (labor, utilities) lower than Japan/US alternatives

## 5.7 The Manufacturing Relocation Dilemma

If AXTI were to build manufacturing outside China:
- **Estimated cost:** $100-200M for a greenfield substrate fab
- **Timeline:** 2-3 years minimum to qualify production
- **Cash available:** $47.8M -- would require significant capital raise
- **Loss of cost advantage:** Manufacturing outside China (US, Japan, SE Asia) would be significantly more expensive
- **Vertical integration risk:** Would they lose access to their China-based Indium refining?

**This is the core strategic dilemma:** The same vertical integration that provides cost advantage is geographically concentrated in China, creating the export control vulnerability.

## 5.8 Key Takeaway

AXTI's vertical integration (especially 7N Indium refining) is a genuine competitive moat that provides meaningful cost advantages. However, this moat is geographically tied to China, making it both a strength and a vulnerability. The cost structure has significant operating leverage -- if volume constraints ease, margins could expand materially. But building equivalent capabilities outside China would be extremely costly and time-consuming.
